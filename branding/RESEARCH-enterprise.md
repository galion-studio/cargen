# Enterprise branding research: cargen / Forge
Researched 2026-10-21 by Grok Bot for Mate. "Observed" means I looked at the live homepage or its HTML on 2026-10-21; everything else is quoted or paraphrased from the linked source. This file contains no taglines; the tagline is Mate's call.

## 1. Five key findings
1. **Enterprise marks are simple and few. None is a picture of the product.** CAE leaders use a wordmark (Siemens) or one geometric sign plus a wordmark (Ansys "Λ", Dassault 3DS, Hexagon). The pictorial marks that win enterprises are each one simple object: Red Hat's hat, MongoDB's leaf, GitLab's tanuki. All four current cargen concepts are illustrated car icons.
2. **Colour is restrained.** Brands use one owned colour on a neutral ground: graphite, navy or black with white or "oat". Siemens uses petrol on deep blue. Databricks uses Lava on Navy and Oat. Linear and Vercel are monochrome, and Stripe's wordmark comes in three colours only. Gradients, starfields and multi-hue art are rare.
3. **CAE imagery is real engineering.** It shows solver output, real cars and products, wireframes and lab photos. Trust comes from evidence: published verification and validation cases (Ansys NAFEMS benchmarks, SimScale's validation library), quantified outcomes, OEM logo walls, analyst badges (Gartner) and certifications (SOC 2, ISO 27001, FedRAMP, and TISAX in automotive).
4. **Emotion still sells in B2B, but the emotion is confidence, not whimsy.** Binet & Field found emotional messaging wins in the long term and rational messaging in the short term. Google/CEB found that personal value, such as confidence and career safety, outweighs business value. Buyers fear the cost of a wrong choice. For an engineering buyer, "nature" should make them feel the work is careful and precise, not cute.
5. **Distinctiveness comes from consistency, and it compounds.** About 95% of B2B buyers are not in the market at any one time, so brand memory built now is what gets recalled later. Assets need fame and uniqueness, which come from using the same codes for years. Forge already has an asset: the charcoal tile with the orange crumple-zone accent. A new brand should build on it, not discard it.

**Recommendation: adjust, not switch.** Drop Earthrise and Grown as the identity. Build one system, "nature as precise engineering": the Wind & Shell mark, simplified, plus the Field Notes plate as the evidence format, inside the existing charcoal and orange codes. Spec in section 5.

## 3. What B2B buyer research says
- **Emotion vs rational.** In the IPA databank, B2B campaigns with emotional strategies outperform rational ones on long-term effects. "Rational messaging is more effective in the short-term." Enterprise buyers "are still influenced by [heuristics], simply because they are human beings." Brand-building and activation budgets should be split roughly 50/50. [B1] [B2]
- **Personal value and risk.** In Google/CEB/Motista's survey of 3,000 B2B purchasers (2013), buyers were "almost 50% more likely" to buy when they saw personal value and "8x more likely to pay a premium". Only 14% would pay a premium for business-value differentiation. [B3] Peter Weinberg (LinkedIn B2B Institute): "There's a much greater cost to the buyer of making a mistake, which means these are more emotional decisions." [B4]
- **Creative quality.** Of 1,600 B2B ads tested by System1 and the B2B Institute, 75% scored one star or less; none scored five. A "fluent device which drives brand recognition" is one of the five-star principles. Commit to one creative idea for a long time. [B4]
- **Mental availability.** Up to 95% of business buyers are out of market at any time, so advertising works by "building and refreshing memory links". Market leaders often reach only about 50% linkage to category entry points. [B5] [B6]
- **Distinctive assets.** An asset's strength is measured by **fame** (it cues the brand unprompted) and **uniqueness** (no competitor shares it). Famous but shared assets should be avoided; new assets start low on both and need sustained investment. [B7] [B8]
- **Descriptive logos.** In the Journal of Marketing Research, logos that describe what the company does improved evaluations and purchase intent, with a weaker effect for familiar brands. [B9] So a still-unknown Forge benefits from a mark that says "car + physics", but drawn simply.
- **Buying complexity.** 77% of B2B buyers rated their last purchase "extremely complex or difficult". Groups of 6 to 10 stakeholders must deconflict information, and "Validation" is a named buying job. Suppliers who provide helpful information see larger deals with less regret. [B10] Forrester puts trust at the centre of B2B buying. [B11]
- **Automotive gate.** OEMs can require suppliers that handle confidential design data or prototypes to hold TISAX labels (AL2/AL3), or in some cases ISO/IEC 27001. [B12] [B13]

## 4. The four concepts against these findings
I looked at each mark at 512 px and 16 px on 2026-10-21. All four are illustrated car icons on a rounded tile; at 16 px each reads as "a car-shaped blob".

| Concept | Enterprise fit | Main risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Earthrise** (navy, aurora arc, stars, glowing dot) | Weakest | Space, aurora gradients and sparkles are common tech imagery, so uniqueness is low [B7]. The stars and arc vanish at 16 px. Orbit/Earth has no tie to crash or aero physics. The look reads consumer or "AI feature", not validated solver. It abandons the existing orange equity |
| **Grown** (Voronoi-cell car, greens) | Weak-to-medium | The cells do look like an FE mesh, which is a real plus. But "mycelium" and fungal growth carry organic and decay associations; the descriptive-logo benefit weakens when associations are negative [B9]. Cells turn to noise at 16 px. Green is far from the current codes |
| **Wind & Shell** (teardrop streamline body, orange crumple bands, charcoal) | **Strongest** | Keeps the charcoal and orange codes and the crumple-zone idea, so it stays continuous with existing assets [B4][B7]. Aero form reads as engineering, and it is the most legible at 16 px. Risks: the teardrop body can read as a toy or concept car, and the "shell" link is implicit |
| **Field Notes** (cream roundel, ruler ticks, star chart, leaf, orange car) | Strong *system*, weak *mark* | The plate/specimen-label idea is the best trust device in the set: provenance on every figure matches Gartner's "validation" job [B10] and the CAE validation-library norm [A3][SS1]. But the roundel holds 6+ elements and turns into an orange dot at 16 px, and the vintage naturalist look reads as heritage or hobby rather than software |

## 5. Recommended direction: "nature as precise engineering"
The principle: nature appears only the way an engineer measures it, as streamlines, shell sections and test corridors, drawn with dimension lines and data. It never appears as scenery, glow or illustration. This keeps the "explore the universe through nature" story Mate wants, while every visual still looks like evidence.

**Mark**
- Start from the Wind & Shell silhouette and cut it to three shapes: one streamline body curve, the two wheels as simple circles, and the orange crumple bands at the nose, reduced from four bands to two or three.
- Keep the charcoal rounded tile as the small-size and app form. This continues the current Forge mark, the only brand asset that already has some exposure.
- Draw it on a 16-unit grid and design a dedicated 16 px version: body plus 2 bands, no inner details.
- One-colour version: the body knocked out of the charcoal tile, bands in orange.
- No stars, leaves, glows or gradients in the mark.

**Distinctive graphic device: "the corridor"**
- Two thin parallel curves form a measured-test tolerance band, the ISO/TS 18571-style corridor the README already uses. A single orange line runs inside it: the simulation result.
- Use it for dividers, banner backgrounds, loading states and section rules.
- It is both a streamline from nature and the project's literal validation promise, and no vendor I reviewed owns it. Treat it as an asset to invest in [B8].

**Palette** (contrast ratios computed against WCAG 2.1)
| Role | Hex | Contrast |
|---|---|---|
| Graphite (ground, text on light) | #16202A (existing) | 14.6:1 on Paper |
| Paper (light ground) | #F4F1EA | — |
| Signal orange: mark, simulation line, validated results only | #F26B1D (existing) | 5.4:1 on Graphite. **2.7:1 on Paper: not for text or thin lines on light** |
| Orange for text and thin lines on light | #B5470C | 4.8:1 on Paper |
| Test-data blue (measured curves) | #2F5F8F on light / #7FA8D6 on dark | 5.9:1 / 6.7:1 |
| Secondary ink | #5A6470 on light / #8A939D on dark | 5.3:1 / 5.3:1 |
| Unvalidated estimate | Dashed or hatched secondary ink, never orange | — |

The rule that makes colour carry meaning: **orange = validated simulation, blue = physical test, grey dashed = unvalidated.** Use it on every graph, so the palette itself becomes the honesty layer.

Uniqueness check: orange and red-orange are used by Databricks, GitLab and Grafana in dev tools [DB1], but the big CAE brands use gold, petrol and blue, so orange on graphite stays ownable in automotive simulation.

**Type** (OFL, so no licences are needed)
- IBM Plex Sans for wordmark and text, IBM Plex Mono with tabular figures for every number, unit and provenance caption (https://github.com/IBM/plex, SIL OFL 1.1).
- Set the wordmark in Plex Sans SemiBold, lowercase or as written, with slightly tightened tracking.
- Plex avoids the Inter/Geist look shared by GitLab, Neural Concept and SimScale (observed).
- Fallback: Inter + JetBrains Mono.

**Imagery**
- Use only Forge's own solver output: meshes, deformation sequences from OpenCourant, pressure and velocity contours, and the validation plots in forge/docs/img.
- Nature appears as technical line drawings of reference specimens (a shell cross-section, streamlines over a body) with dimension lines.
- No stock photos, no AI-generated art (consistent with Red Hat's rule [RH1] and with the project's honesty stance), no space scenery.

**Field Notes plate, kept as the evidence format**
- Every figure sits on a plate with a Plex Mono caption block: source · solver + version · mesh · error vs test (ISO/TS 18571 score), or the label "unvalidated estimate".
- This is the Field Notes idea without the vintage roundel, and it turns the README's truth-layer rule into a visible brand asset.

**Trust architecture for the future commercial site**
- A public validation library page, modelled on SimScale's cases and Ansys's NAFEMS chapter [SS1][A3].
- Quantified, cited results only.
- Customer and OEM logos only when they are real.
- Plan for ISO 27001 and TISAX before selling to OEMs [B12][B13].
- Analyst badges later, if earned.
